## Lessons Learned
Key insights from the day.

A solenoid is an electromagnetic actuator. When its coil is energized, the resulting magnetic field can move a ferromagnetic plunger.

In a clean-agent suppression system, that mechanical movement may operate a release mechanism or pilot valve, depending on the equipment design.

The distinction is:
- The solenoid actuator converts an electrical command into mechanical movement.
- The release mechanism or valve assembly controls the discharge path.
- The stored pressure of the suppression agent provides the energy that drives the agent through the distribution system.
So the solenoid generally doesn't generate the pressure that expels the agent. It initiates the mechanism that allows the pressurized agent to discharge.

That's why observing the disconnected solenoid actuate during the functional test was meaningful: it demonstrated a portion of the electrical-to-mechanical release chain without requiring an actual agent discharge.

The exact arrangement varies by system manufacturer and design.
