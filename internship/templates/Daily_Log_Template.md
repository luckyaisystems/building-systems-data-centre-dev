## 5. Technical Understanding
A solenoid is an electromagnetic actuator. When its coil is energized, the
resulting magnetic field moves a ferromagnetic plunger.

In a clean-agent suppression system, that mechanical movement operates a
release mechanism or pilot valve, depending on the equipment design. The
distinction is:

- The solenoid actuator converts an electrical command into mechanical movement.
- The release mechanism or valve assembly controls the discharge path.
- The stored pressure of the suppression agent provides the energy that
  drives the agent through the distribution system.

Therefore, the solenoid does not generate the pressure that expels the
agent — it initiates the mechanism that allows the pressurized agent to
discharge. That's why observing the disconnected solenoid actuate during
the functional test was meaningful: it demonstrated the electrical-to-
mechanical portion of the release chain without requiring an actual agent
discharge. The exact arrangement varies by manufacturer and system design.
